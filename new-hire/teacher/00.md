# Teacher notes, stop 0: Watch it work

## What the student should leave with

- The task in their own words, and the layout of the cell.
- Able to run `run_cell` with flags and read its table, including `--phases`.
- The difference between sensor data and truth, with an example from the table.
- Simulated time against wall time; what a seed is.

## Lab results

Measured 2026-11-24 at commit `a091cc2` on a 64-bit ARM Linux machine. Run them again
before quoting them.

1. `--pretty --nominal --modules 2`: both placed. Cycle 32.78 s and 26.06 s. Errors
   0.10 and 0.03 mm along, 0.14 and 0.31 mm across. With `--phases`: the first cycle's
   look step takes 3.57 s, because the first picture, taken with the arm at home, shows
   the arm in front of the stack ("no rectangle where the arm stood, taken again from
   the look pose"); the second cycle's look takes 0.00 s, because it uses the picture
   taken at the end of the first cycle (look ahead). To standoff is 5.61 s against
   2.65 s for the same reason: the first starts from the look pose.
2. `--pretty --seed 3 --modules 1 --phases`: pallet off by 3.05 mm, -25.76 mm and
   1.25 degrees. Placed, 0.04 mm along and 0.09 mm across, cycle 32.79 s. Longest
   steps: to standoff 5.61 s, look 3.57 s, swing 2.98 s, return 2.97 s.
3. The same with `--blind`: failed after 6.89 s, "blocked in touch glass: the tool tip
   stopped 4.3 mm short (joint 3 3.9 mrad off)". Peak torque 0.972 of the limit
   (0.609 with the camera). Stack disturbed by 0.853 mm (0.005 mm with the camera).
   The cups met the glass early because the glass was not where the drawing said, and
   the arm pushed until the script noticed it had fallen behind its plan.
4. `--pretty --nominal --modules 1 --blind`: placed, 0.03 mm along, 0.09 mm across. The
   drawing is right, so not looking costs nothing.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What is a cycle, and what are the two numbers a cell is judged by?
   A good answer covers: pick to place to index; cycle time and placement error; that
   both are needed (fast and wrong is no use).
2. *Locate.* In the table from run 2, which columns could a real cell print?
   A good answer covers: seed, result, slot, cycle, carry time, planner time, reason.
   Not the pallet's offset, not the placement error, not the finder's error.
   If stuck, point at: the comment at the top of `src/cell.h` ("Allowed" and "True...").
3. *Explain.* The pallet in run 3 was off by +3.05 mm in x, -25.76 mm in y and 1.25
   degrees. The tool stopped 4.3 mm short of where it meant to. Which of the three did
   that?
   A good answer covers: not y, which slides the glass sideways along itself. Not x
   either: +x is away from the arm, so alone it would have left the cups 3 mm further
   from the glass, still within the grip's 10 mm reach. It was the turn: the tool's end
   cups sit 0.45 m from its centre (`models/tool.xml`), and 1.25 degrees over 0.45 m
   brings one end of the glass about 10 mm nearer. An angle times a distance is the sum
   this course keeps coming back to.
4. *Explain.* Why does the script fail the blind cycle rather than push on and grip?
   A good answer covers: the modules stand free; pushing one presses the whole stack;
   the script allows 2 mm (`PickPlaceOptions::touch_tolerance`) and no more.
   If stuck, point at: `touch_tolerance` and `blocked_tracking_error` in
   `src/pick_place.h`.
5. *Pitfall.* A colleague says "run_cell took 5 seconds, so the cycle time is 5 s".
   What is wrong?
   A good answer covers: wall time against simulated time; the cycle column.
6. *Transfer.* In a language you know, what is the nearest thing to a Bazel target and
   to `MODULE.bazel`?
   A good answer covers: a package or crate or build script entry; a lock file or
   `go.mod` or `package.json`.
7. *Explain.* Why is the second cycle about 6.7 s quicker than the first?
   A good answer covers: look ahead; the arm in the first picture.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "The simulator is the robot's brain." It is the world. The brain is `pick_place` and
  what it calls.
- "The camera makes placement accurate." It makes the pick possible; what it costs and
  what it cannot see come at stops 6 and 11.
- "Blind is broken." It is the baseline that shows what sensing is worth.
- Treating the stack disturbance column as unimportant. A disturbed stack is the next
  pick's problem.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why is the camera 4 m up?* The comment on the camera in `models/cell.xml`: the arm
  stands between a lower camera and the pallet, and at 4 m nothing the arm does reaches
  it. The price is a slanting view.
- *Why does the tube move rather than the arm?* So that every module goes down in the
  same spot; see the top of `src/conveyor.h`.
- *What is the torque tube for?* It is the axis of a solar tracker; the rails hold the
  modules to it. The repository does not say more; this is a question for their team.
- *Is 3 mm good?* The tolerance is the owner's assumption, not a tracker maker's figure
  (`docs/gate.md`). A good question for the team's list.
