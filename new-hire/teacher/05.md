# Teacher notes, stop 5: Pose to joint angles

## What the student should leave with

- Forward kinematics has one answer; inverse has none, one or several.
- The Jacobian as "how the tip moves per joint", and the solve as repeated small
  linear steps.
- Singularities, and damping as the trade that survives them.
- Branches, seeds, and why a path is solved waypoint by waypoint.
- How to read a failed solve: which status, how close, how far from the seed.

Use their own arm: touch the desk with a fingertip and move the elbow without moving
the finger (several answers for one pose); stretch the arm straight and try to push
the fingertip further out (a singularity).

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* Why can forward kinematics be a formula and inverse kinematics here a
   search?
   A good answer covers: forward is a chain of products, one answer; inverse asks
   which angles, through sines and cosines, with several answers or none.
2. *Explain.* What is in the third column of the Jacobian?
   A good answer covers: how the tip moves (three numbers, metres per radian) and
   turns (three numbers) when joint 3 alone turns a little; recomputed at each guess.
   If stuck, point at: the comment on `Kinematics` and `Evaluate` in `src/ik.cc`.
3. *Predict.* (Before run 2.) With five times the damping, which targets suffer?
   A good answer covers: those that need motion in a nearly lost direction, so poses
   beside a singularity; ordinary poses barely notice.
4. *Locate.* Where does the solver decide a step was bad, and what does it do?
   A good answer covers: `cost_new < cost` in `SolveIk`; else the step is dropped,
   damping times `kDampingUp`, and `kStalled` once past `kMaxDamping`.
5. *Explain.* Run 3 printed "jumped branch" and nothing jumped. What does the status
   really test?
   A good answer covers: the largest joint change from the seed against a limit; a
   branch jump is one cause, coarse spacing or a tight limit another.
6. *Pitfall.* A colleague seeds every solve from home "to be consistent". What
   happens along a straight approach?
   A good answer covers: each waypoint may land on a different branch or stall;
   run 1's "from home" 90%; `SolveIkPath` seeds each from the one before.
7. *Transfer.* What is the nearest thing to `IkReport* report = nullptr` in a
   language you know?
   A good answer covers: Go's second return value, an optional keyword argument that
   collects diagnostics, an exception's message; here the caller opts in.
8. *Transfer.* MoveIt reports "63% of the Cartesian path achieved". Which function
   here gives the same kind of answer, and what would you check first?
   A good answer covers: `SolveIkPath` with `IkPathReport::failed_waypoint`; then
   the status at that waypoint: reach, a joint limit, or the step guard.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "A singularity is a place the arm cannot reach." It can be there; it cannot move
  in one direction from there.
- "Damping makes the answer less accurate." It makes each step shorter; the answer
  still meets the tolerance or the solve fails.
- "There is one right set of angles." There are usually several, and which one is a
  decision.
- "Stalled means out of reach." Also a joint limit between the seed and the answer.

## Questions the student may ask

- *This arm has a closed form. Why search?* The top of `src/ik.h` says only that the
  inverse "has no simple formula to read off". A closed form does exist for an arm
  whose last three axes meet at a point, with up to eight branches, and it is not
  simple. Likely reasons to search (reasoning, not in the repository): the same code
  serves any arm in a MuJoCo model, with no second copy of the geometry to keep in
  step, and a seeded search stays on a branch for free. An analytic solver is a fair
  entry for the notebook's ideas list.
- *Why sum position and angle errors as if they were the same unit?* The comment in
  `SolveIk` where `cost` is defined: with a tool about half a metre from the wrist, a
  millimetre and a milliradian are worth about the same.
- *Where is the arm closest to a limit?* Run 1 prints it: at the place, joint 1 is
  5.0 degrees from its limit; the pick's smallest margin over all modules and pallet
  poses is 19.5 degrees ("pick: smallest margin over all").
