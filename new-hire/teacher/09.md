# Teacher notes, stop 9: Finding a path

## What the student should leave with

- Configuration space: one point is one pose of the whole arm and module.
- RRT-Connect, well enough to draw one turn, and what its promise leaves out.
- Why paths are shortened, why four seeds, and what neither can fix.
- The margin budget as an addition they can redo for any setting.
- That a planner is judged over many seeds and several problems, in queries first.
- What it takes to pose two planners the same problem, and that OMPL's RRT-Connect
  then returns this planner's paths.

The budget is what transfers to the job: a student who can say "these four numbers
add up to this margin, and each rests on that assumption" can audit any planner.

2. `print_rrt -- --stats` (100 seeds each): `gap_0.2` found 91 of 100 and

   1.01 to 1.09 times. In arm_like the mean length halves, 18.89 to 9.50.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What is one point of configuration space, and why can the post's
   forbidden region not be written down?
   A good answer covers: six angles, the whole arm and module at once; the region
   depends on every link's and the module's shape at every pose; only "is this point
   free" can be asked.
2. *Explain.* Draw one turn of RRT-Connect.
   A good answer covers: random sample, nearest node, one step if free (`Extend`); the
   other tree steps toward the new node until it arrives or is blocked (`Connect`);
   swap. If stuck, point at: the loop at the bottom of `PlanRrtConnect`.
3. *Predict.* (Before lab 1's second run.) What will the planner say about a goal
   inside a closed ring?
   A good answer after the run covers: `kIterationLimit`, not "impossible"; a real
   cell must treat "not found" as a stop with a reason, which stop 11 does.
4. *Explain.* Where does each of the 45 mm come from, and what would make each term
   wrong?
   A good answer covers: 20 required; 15 is half the edge resolution, sound only if
   the weights really bound the lever arms; 5 for corners, only if the path is timed
   with the reported `max_deviation`; 5 for tracking, only if the follower keeps to
   it (the header says 2.5 mm was measured).
5. *Predict.* (Before lab 3.) Halve the resolution: what happens to the margin, the
   queries and the clearance of the paths?
   A good answer covers: 37.5 mm; about twice the queries; paths may come closer,
   because they are allowed to.
6. *Locate.* Where is the wrist kept from winding up, and what would a path look like
   without it?
   A good answer covers: `PlanMove`, the loop over `kWristJoints` using `wrist_slack`;
   without it a path could turn joint 6 a whole turn, which the metric counts as long
   but shortening cannot always undo.
7. *Pitfall.* A test plans a hard problem with a limit in seconds. It passes on your
   machine and fails now and then on a slower one. What is going on?
   A good answer covers: a limit in seconds makes the outcome depend on the machine;
   bound iterations or queries; `RrtOptions::max_seconds` says so.
8. *Transfer.* You want to know whether OMPL's RRTConnect is better than this one.
   Name three things you must hold equal, and say how this repository did each.
   A good answer covers: any three rows of "How the two were made equal" in
   `docs/ompl-comparison.md`: the box (a `RealVectorStateSpace` with the planner's
   bounds), the metric (coordinates scaled by the weights), the edge check (a motion
   validator that calls `EdgeIsFree`), the step (OMPL's range), the seeds, the limits
   (queries, not a timer thread), exact solutions only. And outside the search: the
   same shortening, the same number of plans, the same compiler flags.
9. *Explain.* (After lab 6.) OMPL's rows carry the same paths as the own search's.
   Does that mean the hand-written search was not worth writing, or that it is right?
   A good answer covers: it is evidence that the search has no defect, since an
   independent implementation agrees plan for plan; the comparison's verdict turned
   on what differs (the straight line first, on the return), not on the carries; and
   the document deliberately does not decide which should be the default.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "RRT finds the shortest path." It finds some path; shortening tightens it.
- "The planner checks the path." It checks points; the margin covers the gaps.
- "A failure means more iterations are needed." Or that no path exists, or that the
  start is inside the margin. `MotionPlanStatus` tells them apart where it can.
- "Planned is safer than scripted." The planned carry passes closer to things, by
  design: 45 mm against about 100 mm (the README says so under `carry.png`).
- "Shortcutting could find the better side." It cannot change sides; seeds can.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why plan afresh for every module?* The scene changes each cycle (the stack
  recedes, the tube fills, the pallet is where the camera saw it), and a few tenths
  of a second of planning run during the lift, so it costs no cycle time. Reuse is a
  fair idea: `docs/ompl-integration.md` proposed `LazyPRM` for exactly that reason.
  The comparison that was run did not include it, and `OmplPlannerNames()` does not
  offer it. A candidate for stop 14's list.
- *Why not RRT\*?* The comment above `ShortenPath`: the free space is mostly open, and
  shortcuts get within a few per cent of the taut string for far fewer queries. The
  comparison now puts a number on it ("Headroom" in `docs/ompl-comparison.md`): the
  best median duration any OMPL planner reached on a carry is 2.6 to 5.0% under the
  own planner's, from planners given up to ten times the queries, in rows that do not
  repeat.
- *If the verdict is "hand-written better", why is OMPL in the build?* The switch is
  what made the comparison possible and keeps it repeatable, and the question of the
  default is open. The cost is in "If OMPL became the default": 82 more modules and a
  build of ten minutes where it took six.
- *Why is the planned carry not the default?* `PickPlaceOptions::carry` says only that
  the earlier tests measure the scripted one. The gate runs the planned one. Whether
  to switch the default is the owner's call.

## Going further

- `PlanRrtConnect` draws its samples with `std::uniform_real_distribution`. Stop 6
  said the library's distributions differ between standard libraries. Ask what "the
  same seed gives the same path" then means, and where it could stop being true. (On
  one toolchain it holds; nothing here has checked another. The agreement with OMPL
  in lab 6 rests on the same thing: `src/ompl_search.h` says both draw one uniform
  number per joint from the same generator.)
- In the comparison's tables, which pair of rows says whether the hand-written search
  has a defect (`own-1` beside `ompl-1`), and which rows ask whether RRT-Connect is
  the right family for this cell (`bitrrt`, `bitstar-1x`, `aorrtc-1x`)?
- Have them run lab 6's BIT* command as the comparison ran it for the row the verdict
  turned on: `--other-way --ompl-queries 25461` and no `--seeds` (fifty), a few times
  over, noting `duration_median_s` against the own search's on that move. The report
  has 1.053 against a limit of 1.05. How often does it fall on the other side?
