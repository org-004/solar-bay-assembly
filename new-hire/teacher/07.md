# Teacher notes, stop 7: The cell and the truth

## What the student should leave with

- The vocabulary of a model file, and able to account for the cell's joints and
  degrees of freedom.
- Which parts are physics (contacts, the servo, the stack settling) and which are
  stand-ins (the weld, the rigid module, the tube swap).
- The truth line: what control code may read, that it is kept by naming, and how to
  check it.
- That a design tolerance is a contract, and what happens just outside it.
- Ownership at the scale of a class: `Cell` owns, others borrow, and a `Cell` moves
  but is never copied.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* Why does the cell have 187 degrees of freedom when the arm has six?
   A good answer covers: every free module has six; the slide has one; the pallet has
   none (mocap); cost of a step grows with all of them.
2. *Explain.* Why is the pallet tilted, not the modules leaned on a level deck?
   A good answer covers: on a slope gravity presses the stack together and into the
   back rest; no friction has to carry a steady load, and MuJoCo's friction creeps.
   If stuck, point at: the second paragraph of `models/pallet.xml`.
3. *Predict.* (Lab 4, before each run.) What will "along" be?
   A good answer after the runs covers: who writes the setting last; sensor against
   count; independent errors. Ask why module 0 is never affected.
4. *Explain.* What does the weld get right about a grip, and what does it leave out?
   A good answer covers: the offset at the moment of the grip is kept and reaches the
   placement; no leak, peel, slip or sag, and no time to build vacuum (the script
   waits `grip_dwell` anyway).
5. *Locate.* Where is the stopping error drawn, where does the script escape it, and
   where does the scorer account for it?
   A good answer covers: `Conveyor::StartStep`; `SensorPosition` in
   `PickPlace::PlanPlace`; `Conveyor::TrueSlotPose` in `MeasurePlacement`.
6. *Pitfall.* In lab 3 a pallet 224 mm off was placed 6.9 mm wrong with no complaint.
   What should be done about it?
   A good answer covers: it is outside the 30 mm the design promises, so first the
   promise must be kept (a fixture, a stop); the software could also notice (points
   at the crop's boundary, an estimate far from the design). Not: widen the crop
   until the test passes.
7. *Explain.* Why can a `Cell` not be copied, and what does `Cell::Load` hand back?
   A good answer covers: `unique_ptr` members; two owners of one `mjData` would be
   wrong; `std::optional<Cell>`, moved out of the function; empty with a message when
   a part is missing.
8. *Pitfall.* Is lab 5's search enough to prove the script never cheats?
   A good answer covers: no. `data()` is writable and could be read; the search for
   `xpos` and `qpos` narrows it; in the end it is review. Ask how they would make it
   stronger.
9. *Transfer.* MoveIt demos often run on ros2_control's mock hardware. What would the
   follower's tracking error be there, and what can that set-up not tell you?
   A good answer covers: zero; nothing about lag, torque limits, contact or sag.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "Contacts are hard and exact." They are stiff springs; the stack moves while settling.
- "The stopping errors pile up over ten slots." Each move aims at an absolute target.
- "The `True...` functions are private." They are public. Only the name and review
  stand between control code and the truth.
- "The grip snaps the module square." It holds whatever offset there was.
- "`const Cell&` copies the cell." It copies nothing, and a `Cell` cannot be copied.

## Questions the student may ask

- *What would change if the suction could slip?* The weld would give way to contact
  with a pressing force, and the script would need to notice. Some of that exists:
  `VacuumHolds`, and the check after pickup refuses a module far from where it should
  be (step 9 at the top of `src/held_module_finder.h`).
- *Could the compiler keep the truth line?* Partly: hand control code a narrower type
  with only the sensor functions, at the cost of a second interface. One for the
  notebook's ideas list.
- *Why 960 constraint rows for 240 contacts?* Four each: by default MuJoCo writes a
  contact with sliding friction as four one-sided constraints. Have them confirm it.
