# Teacher notes, stop 6: Seeing

## What the student should leave with

- Pixel plus depth to a point in the camera's frame, then into the world through the
  camera's pose; and that the pose is the calibration.
- Why three random points, why supporters are counted, and why least squares comes last.
- Averaging: tens of thousands of noisy points give an answer far finer than one of them.
- Refusal as a designed result, with a reason, not a failure of the code.
- That the two errors behave differently: noise averages away, calibration does not.
- Lambdas with their captures; NaN; why a seed repeats and where it might not.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What is the difference between depth and range, and why does the image
   hold depth?
   A good answer covers: along the viewing axis against along the pixel's ray; depth
   times a per-pixel direction is the point; a flat wall square to the camera has one
   depth and many ranges.
2. *Predict.* (Before run 4.) The camera's pose is believed one milliradian off in
   tilt. How far off is the module's estimated position, and in which direction?
   A good answer covers: 1 mrad times about 3.8 m is 3.8 mm; across the line of sight,
   so partly along the glass's normal and partly up the lean; larger for modules
   deeper in the stack because they are farther away.
   If stuck, point at: `Perturbed` in `src/camera_model.h`, and stop 2's sum.
3. *Explain.* Without noise, why is the long-side error 0.2 mm and the others zero?
   A good answer covers: the ends are depth steps located on the pixel grid; counting
   dots corrects it on average, not exactly.
   If stuck, point at: "What counting cannot fix" under
   `RefineOptions::correct_edge_bias`.
4. *Explain.* The floor has more points in the picture than the glass. Why does RANSAC
   not return the floor?
   A good answer covers: the crop removes most of it; the direction constraint
   (`RansacOptions::expected_normal`) skips samples facing the wrong way before they
   are counted. Without both, the biggest plane wins.
5. *Locate.* Where does the finder decide that something is in front of the glass, and
   what does the cycle do about it?
   A good answer covers: `CheckView` and `ViewOptions::max_blocked_fraction`; the look
   step moves the arm to the look pose and takes one more picture (the top comment of
   `src/pick_place.h`, "look").
6. *Pitfall.* `Eigen::Vector2d(UniformUnit(rng), UniformUnit(rng))`: what is wrong?
   A good answer covers: the order of the two calls is the compiler's choice, and it
   differs between x86-64 and ARM, so x and y swap; one draw per statement.
7. *Locate.* Search `src/` for `std::normal_distribution` and
   `std::uniform_int_distribution`. Which results would change under a different
   standard library?
   A good answer covers: `FitPlaneRansac`, `Conveyor::StartStep`, `src/rrt.cc`,
   `src/point_generator.cc` still use library distributions. They agree today because
   the development machines and CI both build with GCC and its library; the
   hand-written draws are where a test's bound depends on particular scenes.
8. *Pitfall.* After run 4 the estimate is 223 of its own sigmas off. What would go
   wrong in code that trusted the sigma?
   A good answer covers: sigma is statistical; a calibration error is the same in
   every picture and invisible to it; only an independent measurement shows it.
9. *Transfer.* In a ROS 2 cell, where would `CameraModel`'s `focal`, `cx`, `cy` and
   `pose` each come from?
   A good answer covers: the camera-info message from the driver; a transform in tf2
   that hand-eye calibration produced.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "Noise is the main error." Here noise costs tenths of a millimetre and a plausible
  calibration error costs millimetres.
- "Four times the pixels, four times the accuracy." Twice: the square root.
- "RANSAC finds the best plane." It finds the most supported of those it tried; the
  refinement afterwards does the fitting.
- "A refusal is a bug to be fixed." Ask what the alternative answer would have been.
- "The camera sees what the collision checker sees." It sees the drawn geometry
  (`DepthOptions::geom_groups`), not the collision hulls.
- "`[&]` is always fine." It is, until the lambda outlives what it refers to.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why rays and not the graphics card?* The top of `src/depth_camera.h`: no display or
  OpenGL context needed, exact geometry, the same result everywhere; the price is 0.2 s
  an image.
- *How does a cell find a calibration error?* Not from one picture. From a second,
  independent look: here the check camera under the held module (stop 11), which sees
  the offset the first error caused. On a real cell, recalibration against a target,
  or placements drifting. A question for their team.
- *Why is the image `float`?* It is what cameras deliver, at half the memory; seven
  digits at 4 m is under a micrometre.
- *Why write this by hand when PCL and OpenCV exist?* The README's first paragraph:
  hand-written so every part can be read and measured. Whether production should do
  the same is for their team; `docs/ompl-integration.md` shows how the repository
  weighs bringing a library in.
