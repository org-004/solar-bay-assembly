# Teacher notes, stop 14: A question of your own

## What the student should leave with

- One question asked, predicted, run and reported in the repository's form.
- The habit of a baseline on the same seeds, and of saying what a result does not
  show.
- A next question.

This stop has two sessions: a short one before any runs, on the question and the
prediction, and a longer one on the page. In both you are a colleague reviewing a
proposal, not an examiner.

## Lab results

The chapter's worked example was measured 2026-11-24 at commit `a091cc2` with

```
bazel run --config=opt //:run_cell -- --pretty --trials 5 --check \
    --calibration 1,0.05 [--check-calibration MM,DEG]
```

for no check-camera error and for 1,0.05, 2,0.10 and 3,0.15. The summary lines gave
the table's figures; "check after pickup: 5 checked (0 retaken), 0 refused (0
implausible)" at every level. The 1,0.05 row's worst (1.74 and 1.39 mm) equals the
README's figure for twenty pallets, so that table's worst cases are among the first
five seeds.

The student's own results are theirs. Rerun one of their commands yourself before the
second session, to see that it gives what their page says.

## Questions to ask

Before the runs:

1. *Explain.* What is the question, in one sentence, and what would a cell do
   differently depending on the answer?
   A good answer covers: one thing varied, one thing measured. If the answer would
   change nothing, it is not yet a question.
2. *Predict.* What do you expect, and what is the reasoning?
   A good answer covers: a direction and a rough size, from something learnt at an
   earlier stop. "I do not know" is acceptable; "it will be better" is not yet a
   prediction.
3. *Pitfall.* What is your baseline, and what stays the same between rows?
   A good answer covers: same seeds, same commit, same machine, same flags but one.
4. *Explain.* How long will it take to run, and how many seeds can you afford?
   A good answer covers: about five seconds of wall time per module with
   `--config=opt`; planning the runs before starting them.

After the runs:

5. *Explain.* What surprised you?
   This is the question that matters. Follow it wherever it goes.
6. *Pitfall.* What does the table not show?
   A good answer covers: at least one of stop 13's kindnesses that bears on this
   result; the number of seeds; anything held fixed that a real cell would vary.
7. *Locate.* Where in the code is the reason for what you saw?
   A good answer covers: a file and a symbol, read, not guessed.
8. *Transfer.* How would you ask this of a production cell?
   A good answer covers: what would stand in for seeds and for truth; what a trial
   would cost.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- A result reported as a single number with no spread.
- "It passed the gate, so it is better." The gate says nothing got worse than the
  tolerance; it does not rank two versions that both pass.
- Wall-clock time reported as the result.
- A conclusion wider than the experiment: five seeds on one pallet do not show "the
  cell is robust to calibration error".
- Treating a negative result as a failure. "It made no difference, and here is why"
  is a finding.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Is my question good enough?* If a run can answer it in two days and the answer
  would change what someone does, yes.
- *Can I commit this?* Not the lab edits. The page can go wherever their team keeps
  such reports; a change to the code is a proposal to the owner.
- *My change makes the cell faster but the gate fails. What now?* That is the finding.
  Report both numbers. Do not touch the tolerance.
- *Can I do the OMPL comparison?* Not as this stop. Its design and its decisions are
  the owner's (`docs/ompl-integration.md`, "Decisions for the owner"). Reading that
  section and writing down what they would ask the owner is a fair preparation.

## Hints for the chapter's menu

Not answers; where to look if the student is stuck on how to begin.

- *Where the seconds go.* `--phases` on a second cycle (the first carries the look
  step's retake). The speeds and waits are in `PickPlaceOptions`; each has a comment
  saying what it protects.
- *Resolution.* The margin budget in the header of `src/motion_planner.h` ties
  `edge_resolution` to the planning margin: halving one changes the other. How large
  the margin can get before the carry's start is refused is in stop 9's teacher notes,
  which found the header's "58 mm" does not hold for `print_plan`'s scene.
- *Seeds.* `MotionPlanOptions::seeds`; shortening cannot change which way round an
  obstacle a path goes, which is what several seeds are for.
- *The envelope.* The finder's crop comes from the designed placement tolerance
  (`kPlacementTolerance`, `CropFromPrior`); the grip reaches 10 mm
  (`SuctionOptions::max_distance`); the touch allows 2 mm
  (`PickPlaceOptions::touch_tolerance`).
- *Blending.* `PathTimingOptions::max_deviation` and its comment on what a radian of
  joint-space deviation is worth at the tool.
- *The deep end.* `lift_back` and the back rest in `src/pick_place.h`; the camera's
  view of module_29 in `models/cell.xml`.
