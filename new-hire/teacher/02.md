# Teacher notes, stop 2: Where things are

## What the student should leave with

- A frame, a pose, and the "B in A" way of naming one.
- Chaining and inverting, and solving a chain for any one factor.
- That the type system cannot tell a right product from a wrong one.
- Angle times distance as a habit of estimation.
- Eigen's types as values; what a view is.

Do not hurry this stop. Have the student draw the frames on paper: the tool tip with
its +Z into the glass, the module with its +z out of it. If they cannot draw
`DesignModuleInTip`, they do not have it yet.

2. Seed 3 with `--calibration 1,0.05`:

   | | along, mm | across, mm |
   |---|---|---|
   | no check (`--carry planned`) | -1.03 | 2.00 |
   | `--check` | -0.06 | -0.00 |
   | `--check`, correction inverted | -2.00 | 4.01 |

   The carry's line with `--phases` reads "grip off the design by +2.01 -0.96 +1.93
   mm, 0.032 deg": x, y, z in the design module's frame. On the rails the module's
   long side (its x) lies across the tube, so the 2.01 shows as "across" and the 0.96
   as "along".

   Why twice. Write the measured grip as `H = D * d`, where `D` is the design grip
   and `d` the small offset. The module lands at `tip * H`.
   - No check: `tip = R`, where `R * D` is the target. It lands at `R * D * d`: off
     by `d`.
   - Right: `tip = R * D * H^-1`. It lands at `R * D`.
   - Inverted: `tip = R * H * D^-1`. It lands at `R * H * D^-1 * H = R * D * d * d`:
     off by `d` twice.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* Say `module_in_tip` in a sentence. What does multiplying a point by it
   do?
   A good answer covers: the module's frame as seen from the tool tip; it turns a
   point's coordinates in the module's frame into coordinates in the tip's.
2. *Locate.* Where is the measured grip turned into a different place for the tool to
   go?
   A good answer covers: `PickPlace::PlanPlace`, the line choosing between
   `CorrectedTipTarget(...)` and `design_release`; the function itself in
   `src/held_module_finder.cc`.
3. *Predict.* (Before the third run of lab 2.) What will the inverted correction give?
   A good answer covers: about (-2, 4), and after the run, the algebra above. A
   common wrong guess is "the same as no check", on the idea that two errors cancel.
4. *Explain.* Why is `DesignModuleInTip` half a turn about x rather than no rotation?
   A good answer covers: the tool's +Z points into the glass and the module's +z out
   of it; x is shared because `GripPose` lays the tool's X along the module's long
   side; turning about x flips y and z.
   If stuck, point at: the comment on `GripPose` in `src/ik.h`.
5. *Pitfall.* Lab 3 compiled. Why did the compiler not object?
   A good answer covers: both products are `Isometry3d * Isometry3d`; the frames are
   in the names, not the types. What caught it was a physical check at run time. Ask
   what would have happened without that check.
6. *Explain.* The pallet camera's calibration is off by 0.05 degrees. Roughly how far
   off is the module's estimated position?
   A good answer covers: 0.05 degrees is 0.87 mrad; times about 4 m is 3 to 4 mm.
   Compare with run 2's uncorrected error of about 2.2 mm (that seed's draw of the
   error's direction).
7. *Transfer.* In tf2, what is the pose this code calls `tool_tip_pose_world`?
   A good answer covers: `lookupTransform("world", <tip frame>)`; that someone must
   publish the tip frame (URDF, or a static transform from the flange); that it has a
   timestamp.
8. *Pitfall.* You read a quaternion out of MuJoCo and hand its four numbers to a ROS
   message in the same order. What happens?
   A good answer covers: w lands in x; a rotation that is wrong but plausible, with
   no error raised.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "A pose is a position." It is a position and an orientation; most hard bugs are in
  the orientation.
- "The inverse of a pose negates the translation." Only if there is no rotation: the
  inverse's translation is `-R^T * t`.
- "`a * b` applies a first." With points in the child's frame on the right, `b` is
  applied first.
- "The check makes the camera's calibration irrelevant." It replaces the pallet
  camera's error with the check camera's own; the README's last table row shows what
  is left.
- Mixing up the tool tip with the flange. The tip is 0.30 m out from the flange
  (`models/tool.xml`).

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why does the grip's offset show as "across" and "along"?* The module's x is its
  long side, which lies across the tube on the rails (`PlacementError` in
  `src/score.h`).
- *Why did the offset's z part not hurt the placement?* It is along the tool's axis.
  Uncorrected, the module is let go about 2 mm higher than planned and drops onto the
  rails; the "height" column still reads zero because it rests on them.
- *How would I tell software from hardware on a real cell?* There is no general
  answer in this repository. Useful directions: does it fail the same way on every
  module (a constant offset points at calibration or code), did it start after a
  change, does a second independent measurement agree. A question for their team.
- *Why not make "pose of X in Y" a type so the compiler can check?* It can be done
  (frame-tagged types) and some libraries do; it costs templates everywhere. A fair
  entry for the notebook's ideas list.
