# Teacher notes, stop 8: Will it hit anything

## What the student should leave with

- A collision check answers for shapes (hulls), for one configuration, against a
  picture of the cell that somebody had to supply.
- Which pairs count, and the two ways a pair is taken out: by role, and by an allowed
  contact for one move.
- "Free" means at least a margin away, and how the margin is got from MuJoCo.
- The sweep bound: lever arm times angle, summed over the joints, and why it turns a
  sample spacing into a guarantee.
- That the held module changes both the geometry and the lever arms.

The sweep bound is the idea to spend time on. Stop 9's margin budget and stop 10's
corner deviation both rest on it.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* The arm has two meshes per link. Which one does the check use, and what
   is a convex hull?
   A good answer covers: the collision mesh (group 3, class `arm_collision`); the hull
   fills in every hollow; fatter than the link, so safe, but it can report "close"
   where the steel is not.
   If stuck, point at: the `arm_collision` comment in `models/arm.xml`.
2. *Locate.* Where is it decided that two modules leaning on each other are not a
   collision?
   A good answer covers: `CollisionChecker::Counts` (both obstacles); and, for speed,
   `ApplyFilters`, which stops MuJoCo pairing them at all.
3. *Predict.* (Before lab 4.) With the filter off, what changes in the table?
   A good answer after the run covers: only the times; the header's claim "changes no
   answer" is a test (`FastModelGivesThePlainModelsAnswers`), and they have now seen
   it.
4. *Explain.* The planner wants 45 mm to the world and 25 mm among the arm's own parts.
   What margin does each geom get?
   A good answer covers: moving geoms 12.5 mm, obstacles 32.5 mm; moving plus obstacle
   is 45, moving plus moving 25; and why `SetMargins` refuses a self margin above
   twice the margin (the obstacle's share would be negative).
   If stuck, point at: `ApplyFilters`, the line that sets `geom_margin`.
5. *Explain.* A path's waypoints are all free. Is the path free?
   A good answer covers: no, only checked at the waypoints; sample the segments; the
   sweep bound says how far anything can move between samples; a margin of half the
   spacing closes the gap.
6. *Predict.* Joint 1 turns by 0.01 rad with a module held. How far can the module's
   far corner move? And joint 6 by the same?
   A good answer covers: about 45 mm and about 13 mm, from the lever arms the header
   of `src/path_timing.h` estimates (4.5 m and 1.3 m). The reasoning, not the digits.
7. *Pitfall.* A module was set down on the tube a minute ago. The planner then plans
   the next carry straight through where it lies. What went wrong?
   A good answer covers: nobody told the checker; the scene is what was put in it.
   In this code the cycle keeps `known_modules` and `CarryScene` places them.
8. *Pitfall.* Two threads call `IsFree` on the same checker. What can happen?
   A good answer covers: both write angles into one scratch `mjData`; one reads the
   other's pose; wrong answers with no crash. One checker per thread.
9. *Transfer.* What are `SetHeldModule` and `AllowContact` called in MoveIt?
   A good answer covers: an attached collision object; an entry in the allowed
   collision matrix (or the object's list of links it may touch).

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "The check knows where things really are." It knows what it was told.
- "A bigger margin is always safer." It also removes poses the arm needs; with a large
  self margin the wrist's own hulls collide (stop 9's lab shows it).
- "The margin is measured." For yes-or-no it is not: geoms are inflated and MuJoCo
  reports contact. Only `Check` with `clearance` measures.
- "Convex hull means bounding box." A hull follows the shape; it only loses hollows.
- "Self-collision is rare." With a 2.3 m module on the wrist, the module against the
  upper arm is the pair that shaped the scripted carry (`turn_outboard` in
  `src/pick_place.h`).

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why not check the real meshes?* Convex shapes have fast, well-understood distance
  algorithms (GJK); arbitrary meshes do not. Other planners make the same trade, some
  with simpler shapes still (spheres, capsules).
- *Why MuJoCo for collision?* The header does not argue it. What it buys is visible:
  planner and physics see the same shapes, and the collision pass runs alone
  (`mj_collision`). The cost is the care in `ApplyFilters`.
- *Why is `margin` 20 mm?* The comment on `CollisionOptions::margin`: half of a 40 mm
  check spacing. It is the owner's figure; changing it is not a lab for a real branch.
- *How close does the real arm come?* `cycle_check` measures it from the truth in
  every run (lab 2). On a real cell nothing can; ask their team what stands in for it.

## Going further

- `Pose` updates the held module's body by hand instead of calling `mj_kinematics` a
  second time. Ask what that saves, and which test would notice if MuJoCo changed how
  it places a free body (`HeldModuleIsPlacedAsKinematicsPlacesIt`).
- The header says the scripted cycle stays "30 mm or more" from what it must not
  touch; lab 2 measures 29.5 mm in the simulated cycle. Ask where half a millimetre
  could come from. (One candidate, not checked: the header's figure is for planned
  angles, and the simulated arm sags and lags a little.)
