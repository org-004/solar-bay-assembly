# Teacher notes, stop 11: The cycle

## What the student should leave with

- The cycle as a state machine ticked once per physics step, and why it cannot wait.
- Which earlier stop each step leans on; able to go from a failed step to the code.
- How a blocked move is noticed from joint angles alone, and where it is allowed.
- What the check after pickup adds, what the plausibility test catches and what it
  cannot, and why the script stops with a reason rather than falling back.
- In C++: reading a large class; an enum and a switch as a state machine; members that
  are optional, references or callbacks, and the contracts they carry.

If time is short, lab runs 1, 3 and 4 matter most.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What happens in one call to `Update`? Why not loop until the arm arrives?
   A good answer covers: notice a reset, `Advance` (is this step over, begin the next),
   then the follower writes the commands; the physics only moves when `Cell::Step` is
   called, so a loop inside would wait for ever.
2. *Locate.* Where does the script decide the arm has run into something, and from
   what data?
   A good answer covers: `MoveFinished`; the follower's `TrackingError` against
   `blocked_tracking_error`, or not arrived `arrive_timeout` after the plan's end;
   joint angles only. If stuck, point at: the comment on `blocked_tracking_error`.
3. *Predict.* (Before lab 2's second run.) With `lift_back` at zero, which joint's
   figure rises, in which step, and is the module still placed?
   A good answer covers: joint 2, in lift; the back rest does not give way; yes, it is
   placed, which is why a table of placement errors alone would never show it.
4. *Explain.* Why is the carry planned at the start of lift? What would a real cell do
   if the planning did not fit?
   A good answer covers: the start pose is already known; computing overlaps motion;
   otherwise the arm stands at the end of clear and the cycle grows by the difference.
5. *Explain.* (After lab 3.) Why was seed 3 placed when five were refused? What would
   catch it?
   A good answer covers: a turn across the camera's view tilts the measured module,
   which the cups cannot do; a turn about its own axis is a yaw, which they can. Only
   a better calibration or an independent measurement catches it; in simulation the
   scorer does, from the truth.
6. *Pitfall.* A colleague proposes: "if the planner finds no path, fall back to the
   scripted carry". What is lost?
   A good answer covers: the failure stops being visible; the scripted carry was never
   checked against the scene that defeated the planner; the check needs the planned
   carry anyway.
7. *Locate.* Name two things in the planner's scene that are beliefs, not
   measurements.
   A good answer covers: the stack's pose (one picture's estimate); that a module let
   go lies exactly in its slot; the held module at the design grip until measured.
   If stuck, point at: `CarryScene` and `ModulePlace`.
8. *Transfer.* How would you write "wait for the arm, then grip" without blocking in
   a language you know? What is the nearest thing in a ROS 2 application?
   A good answer covers: a callback, a promise or `await`; an action's result
   callback, or a behaviour tree node returning "running" each tick.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "The script moves the arm." It asks the follower for a move; the follower writes
  commands; the physics moves the arm.
- "Fits" means the plan is good. It means the computing took less time than the
  motion it ran beside.
- "Done means placed in tolerance." It means the script finished. The verdict is the
  scorer's (stop 12).
- "The check makes the placement exact." It exchanges the pallet camera's error for
  the check camera's own.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why does the carry start at the end of clear?* At the end of pull back the module
  is still within the planner's 45 mm planning margin of the stack; the header says so
  under "The planned carry".
- *What happens to a module left on the tool?* It stays. `Start` refuses a cycle that
  begins with the pick while the vacuum holds. Tests let it go by hand
  (`tests/check_cycle_test.cc`); a real cell needs an answer, and there is none here.
- *Is `mutable` needed on `scratch_`?* Not for the compiler (it builds without):
  `const` on a method protects the handle, not what it points at. It is for the reader.
- *Why has the switch in `Advance` no `default:`?* So that the compiler warns when a
  step is added and not handled there; a default would swallow it silently.
