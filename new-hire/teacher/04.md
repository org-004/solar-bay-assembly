# Teacher notes, stop 4: Motors

## What the student should leave with

- A position servo as spring and brake, and why its command must move smoothly.
- Lag as a fixed time behind, and the one-line cure.
- Feedforward: torque from a model, so that feedback has little left to do.
- Planned against measured; tracking error as a signal, not only a fault.
- That a message such as "blocked" is the script's conclusion, not an observation.

If they have no physics, spend time on torque = inertia times acceleration with a door
as the example. Skip the mass matrix's details; "the torque each joint needs depends on
the pose and on what the other joints are doing" is enough.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* Why not write each joint's goal angle into `ctrl` and wait?
   A good answer covers: the stiff spring saturates the motor; overshoot; no control
   over the path in between; the plan from stop 3 is there to be followed.
   If stuck, point at: "Two things a controller has to know" in `models/arm.xml`.
2. *Predict.* (Before run 1's "without" line.) How far does joint 1 trail at 1.24
   rad/s with a lag of 0.0355 s? And at the tool, 2 m out?
   A good answer covers: 44 mrad; about 9 cm. Angle times distance again.
3. *Locate.* Where are the two corrections added to the command?
   A good answer covers: `ArmFollower::Update`: `command += arm_.lag.cwiseProduct(
   plan.velocity)`, then the loop adding `(torque_[dof] + data->qfrc_bias[dof]) / kp`.
4. *Explain.* Run 2 says "blocked". Was the arm blocked?
   A good answer covers: no; the test for "blocked" is tracking error over 3 mrad,
   and a follower without lag compensation exceeds that by itself. The threshold
   assumes a follower that tracks to about 0.1 mrad.
5. *Explain.* Why did turning feedforward off move the placement along the tube?
   A good answer covers: sag at the pick, frozen into the grip, the module's short
   side along the tube. Give them run 3's `--check` line if they are stuck.
6. *Pitfall.* A follower is reused after `Cell::Reset`. What goes wrong?
   A good answer covers: the plan's times are in the old clock; `TimeLeft` and
   `Planned` are meaningless. `PickPlace::NoticeReset` exists for this.
   If stuck, point at: "First use" in the comment at the top of `src/arm_follower.h`.
7. *Transfer.* `std::visit` with a lambda over a `std::variant`: how would you write
   the same thing in a language you know?
   A good answer covers: an interface with `At` and `duration` and two classes; or a
   tagged union with a `match`. Here there is no base class and no run-time lookup.
8. *Explain.* The follower's model does not include the held module. What does that
   cost, and why not just add it?
   A good answer covers: about half a milliradian of sag, half a millimetre at the
   tip; the weld is a constraint between separate bodies; a real controller is told
   the payload and has the same problem when the figure is wrong.
9. *Transfer.* In the MoveIt cells, what plays the part of `Done` and of
   `blocked_tracking_error`?
   A good answer covers: the trajectory controller's goal and path tolerances; the
   robot controller's own following-error and collision monitoring.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "Lag is a delay in the signal." Nothing is delayed; it is the steady error of a
  spring working against a brake.
- "Feedforward replaces feedback." It leaves feedback less to do; feedback still
  corrects whatever the model gets wrong.
- "0.9 is slower, so it is safer in every way." It is a torque margin for the worst
  move. It does nothing for a wrong pose.
- "`Done` means the plan's time is up." It also needs the arm to have arrived.
- "The variant holds both." It holds one, and remembers which.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why is there no integral term?* MuJoCo's position actuator has none (see the
  `<position>` elements in `models/arm.xml`). What an integrator would do, removing
  the steady error under gravity, the feedforward does here. This is reasoning, not
  something the repository states: an integrator would also keep pushing harder
  against an obstacle, which is the opposite of what the blocked test wants.
- *How do I tell "blocked" from a collision on a real cell?* The repository cannot
  say. Directions: does it happen at the same place in free air, on every joint in
  proportion to its speed (lag), or only on contact. A question for their team.
- *What are armature and damping?* The comment at joint 1 in `models/arm.xml`: the
  motor's rotor seen through the gearbox, and a stand-in for gearbox friction. Both
  are estimates; the README says KUKA publishes neither.
- *Why `dampratio="1"`?* Critical damping: the brake is just strong enough that the
  joint does not ring. The comment above `<actuator>` explains the pose it is set at.
