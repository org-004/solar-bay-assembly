# Teacher notes, stop 3: Moving one axis

## What the student should leave with

- Position, speed, acceleration and jerk as a chain of rates, and what limits each.
- The trapezoid and the S-curve, and the three time formulas for a long move.
- A joint move as a straight line in joint space, paced by its slowest joint.
- That a profile is a function of time, planned once and sampled.
- A class with a private constructor and factories that return `std::optional`.

Have them draw the three graphs of a trapezoid (position, speed, acceleration against
time) before they run anything. If the acceleration graph comes out as a ramp, go back
to "rate of change".

## Lab results

Measured 2026-11-24 at commit `a091cc2`. Run them again before quoting them.

1. `print_profile -- --pretty`: "trapezoid 1.1854 s, S-curve 1.2854 s". By hand:
   1.5708 / 2 + 2 / 5 = 1.1854, and the S-curve adds 5 / 50 = 0.1. The S-curve's jerk
   column reads 50 for the first 0.1 s, then 0 while acceleration holds at 5.
2. `print_profile -- --pretty 0.05 2 5 50`: "trapezoid 0.2000 s, S-curve 0.3175 s".
   Neither reaches 2 rad/s: the trapezoid peaks at 0.5 (printed 0.49, between samples)
   and the S-curve at 0.31. The S-curve does not reach 5 rad/s² either (3.97): it is
   all ramps, four of them, each `cbrt(d / (2 j))` = 0.0794 s long. By hand the
   trapezoid is `2 * sqrt(0.05 / 5)` = 0.2. The S-curve loses 59% here against 8% in
   run 1: jerk costs most on short moves.
3. Quarter turn, last number changed: jerk 500 gives 1.1954 s, 5000 gives 1.1864 s,
   5 gives 2.1582 s. The first two fit `1.1854 + a / j`. The third does not (the
   formula says 2.185): at jerk 5 the ramp never reaches 5 rad/s², so it is run 2's
   case again, `4 * cbrt(1.5708 / 10)` = 2.158.
4. `run_cell --pretty --nominal --modules 1 --phases`: `swing` 2.966 s and `return`
   2.966 s; cycle 32.78 s; "peak torque fraction: whole cycle worst 0.609". By hand,
   with joint 1's 2.0384 rad/s and 1.98271 rad/s² and a ramp of 0.1 s:
   pi / 2.0384 + 2.0384 / 1.98271 + 0.1 = 2.669 s at full speed, and 2.669 / 0.9 =
   2.966.
5. With `kJerkPerAcceleration` changed in `src/arm.cc`:

   | jerk multiple | `swing`, s | cycle, s | peak torque fraction | along / across, mm |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 10 (as built) | 2.966 | 32.78 | 0.609 | 0.10 / 0.14 |
   | 100 | 2.866 | 29.10 | 0.612 | 0.10 / 0.14 |
   | 2 | 3.412 | 44.21 | 0.551 | 0.10 / 0.14 |

   Ten times the jerk saves 3.7 s a cycle and changes nothing else the table shows.
   The gain is far more than the 0.1 s on `swing` because the cycle is mostly short
   moves, which is run 2's lesson again. It looks free because the simulated arm and
   module are rigid (the README's "Where the simulation is kinder": "The module is
   rigid"). What a real cell would pay in ringing and wear, the simulation cannot say.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What is jerk, and why limit it when the motors do not care?
   A good answer covers: the rate of change of acceleration, so of force; springy
   structure rings when force comes on suddenly; it is a chosen limit, not KUKA's.
   If stuck, point at: the comment on `limits` in `src/arm.h`.
2. *Predict.* (Before run 2.) Over 0.05 rad, which is slower relative to its
   trapezoid: this move or the quarter turn?
   A good answer covers: the short one by far; ramps are a fixed cost per speed
   change; the cycle has many short moves.
3. *Locate.* Where is it decided which joint sets the pace of a joint move?
   A good answer covers: `FractionLimit` in `src/joint_move.cc`, called three times
   in `JointMove::Plan`; a joint that does not move is skipped.
4. *Explain.* In `JointMove::At`, why does the end return `goal_` and not
   `start_ + delta_ * 1.0`?
   A good answer covers: floating point; the two can differ in the last bit; the next
   move starts from this value and a test compares it exactly. Stop 1's 2501 steps
   again.
5. *Pitfall.* `Profile p = *Profile::SCurve(d, limits);` compiles. What is wrong?
   A good answer covers: no test for the empty case; with a zero or negative limit it
   is undefined behaviour, not an exception. Where the repository does this (for
   example `*JointMove::Plan(...)` in a test) the inputs are known good.
6. *Transfer.* What is the nearest thing to `std::optional<Profile>` in a language
   you know, and to a private constructor with a static factory?
   A good answer covers: `Optional`, `T | None`, `(value, ok)`; a named constructor
   or factory function; that the point is no half-made object.
7. *Predict.* A move is replayed at `speed = 0.5`. How do its duration, its peak
   acceleration and its peak jerk change?
   A good answer covers: twice as long; a quarter; an eighth; the path is the same.
   If stuck, point at: the comment on `MoveOptions::speed` in `src/arm_follower.h`.
8. *Explain.* Why does the cycle not make every move a single joint move?
   A good answer covers: the tool bows off the straight line by about
   `2.2e-4 * L^2` mm; fine in open space, not when the cups approach glass.
9. *Transfer.* In the MoveIt cells, what plays the part of `Profile::At`?
   A good answer covers: the trajectory controller interpolating between timed
   points; or the robot controller's own interpolator for PTP and LIN.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "The S-curve is smoother, so it is always better." It is slower; the question is
  what the machine needs.
- "All joints move at their own top speed." Only the pacing joint is at a limit.
- "`const` on a member function means it returns a constant." It means the function
  does not change the object.
- "An empty optional throws when you use it." Not with `*` or `->`.
- "The trapezoid has no jerk." It has unbounded jerk at the instants its acceleration
  jumps; the code reports 0 everywhere (the comment on `Profile::At`).

## Questions the student may ask

- *What sets the conveyor's acceleration limit?* Friction: the modules ride on the
  tube by friction alone. The comment on `ConveyorSettings::limits` in
  `src/conveyor.h` does the sum.
- *How would I choose the jerk limit for a real cell?* The repository does not say.
  Directions: the lowest resonance of what is being moved, what the gearbox maker
  allows, and measurement on the cell. A question for their team's list.
- *Why does `Append` set the acceleration and not carry it over?* Its comment: the
  trapezoid's acceleration jumps, and for the S-curve setting it stops rounding error
  building up across segments.
- *Why are there seven segments and not a formula?* Each segment has constant jerk,
  so position is a cubic in time inside it (`Advance`). Seven cubics joined end to end
  is the formula.
