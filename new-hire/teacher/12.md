## What the student should leave with

- A trial, a verdict, the gate; that the tolerance is the owner's decision.
- What "no failures in n" does and does not show, and how to count independent tries.

## Questions to ask

1. *Explain.* What are a trial, a verdict and the gate? Who decided on 3 mm?
   A good answer covers: a seeded pallet and its cycles; in or out of tolerance, with
   failed and unconfirmed cycles out; eight whole pallets; the owner, as an
   assumption.
2. *Predict.* (Before lab 2.) Will the two files be identical? If not, where?
   A good answer covers: every simulated number identical; only measured wall times
   differ. If stuck, point at: the paragraph "Replays are deterministic" in
   `docs/gate.md`.
3. *Explain.* The gate placed 240 of 240. What failure rate does that rule out? How
   many independent tries were there?
   A good answer covers: about 3/240, or 1.25%, at 95%; but one calibration draw per
   pallet, so eight tries of that; and nothing about what the simulation leaves out.
4. *Locate.* Where is the one place the wall clock can change what the script does?
   A good answer covers: the planner's time budget (`MotionPlanOptions::time_budget`,
   10 s against a few tenths used). If stuck, point at: the comment on `max_seconds`
   in `src/rrt.h`.
5. *Locate.* In `RunTrials`, which variables can more than one thread touch, and what
   protects each?
   A good answer covers: `next` and `load_failed` are atomics; `load_error` is written
   under `load_mutex`; `results` is written one slot per trial; `done` is called under
   `done_mutex`; `trials` is only read. The `Cell` is the thread's own.
6. *Pitfall.* A colleague adds a test: "planning the carry takes under 400 ms". What
   happens, and what should it say?
   A good answer covers: it fails on a loaded machine with nothing wrong; bound the
   collision queries. If stuck, point at: "No bound on the median wall time" in
   `tests/motion_planner_test.cc`.
7. *Explain.* Why does the failure file say `along_tube = 0.006139602026010544` and
   not 6.14 mm?
   A good answer covers: the shortest text that reads back to the same `double`;
   `CompareFailure` compares exactly; rounded text could only say "close".
8. *Pitfall.* After your fix a replay prints "NOT the same failure" and exits with 2.
   Are you done?
   A good answer covers: read the differences. "No bad cycle" is a fix; a different
   step or reason is a new failure. Then run the gate.
9. *Transfer.* What would it take to replay a failure from a production cell?
   A good answer covers: recorded sensor data, the software version, the calibration
   files, the planner's seeds; and that the world itself cannot be reset. A question
   for their team: what is recorded today?

## Misconceptions to listen for

- "Deterministic means correct." It means repeatable. A wrong answer repeats too.
- "More trials of the same kind cover more." They narrow the same estimate; they do
  not try anything new.
- "Threads change the results." Not here, by construction; `ThreadsDoNotChangeATrial`
  in `tests/trial_runner_test.cc` asserts it.
- "Cached PASSED means it just ran."
- "The cycle check is part of the controller." It reads the truth; it is a judge.

## Questions the student may ask

- *Why eight pallets?* Time: a whole pallet is about two minutes of one core; see the
  comment on `kGateTrials` in `src/trial_runner.h`.
- *Why has `gate_test.cc` its own `main`?* To take `--save-failures`, and to switch
  off GoogleTest's own sharding so that the test can deal its trials out itself.
- *Why is the gate left out of `--config=agent`?* It is heavy; one run, alone on the
  machine, before code lands (`.bazelrc`, and "Build and test" in the root
  `AGENTS.md`).
- *Is `profile_test`'s random test the same on every platform?* Yes. It uses the
  generator's raw outputs, not a library distribution, and its three draws sit in a
  braced list, which C++ evaluates left to right, unlike function arguments.
